Research

My research develops GPU-based nonlinear programming methods for dynamic optimization, where differential-algebraic equation (DAE) constraints are discretized into large, structured nonlinear programs. I implement these methods as open-source Julia software, built on ExaModels.jl and MadNLP.jl. These methods are then applied to parameter estimation and optimal control problems across a variety of fields in process systems engineering.

Scalable Dynamic Optimization with GPUs

Dynamic optimization problems can be solved with sequential methods, which integrate the DAEs with a time-stepping solver, or with simultaneous methods, which discretize the DAEs over the full horizon into a single large nonlinear program. The repetitive structure of simultaneous formulations maps naturally onto GPUs. I develop ExaModelsCollocation.jl to formulate these problems with orthogonal collocation, and study how GPU computing changes the trade-offs between sequential and simultaneous methods [C1].

Simultaneous methods require a sufficiently fine and properly distributed discretization mesh for accurate solution profiles. However, the mesh layout must be chosen prior to solving the problem. I am developing parametric sensitivity-based adaptive mesh relocation strategies for GPU-based nonlinear solvers, which use the sensitivities to efficiently update state trajectories upon relocating mesh nodes, building on ParametricNLPModels.jl.

I am also developing GPU-based batched interior-point methods for solving weakly coupled multi-scenario nonlinear programs.

Estimation and Control Applications

I apply these methods to parameter estimation and optimal control problems in process systems engineering, including systems biology and energy systems. For example, ExaModelsPEtab.jl formulates parameter estimation problems from systems biology as nonlinear programs that can be solved efficiently on GPUs.
